FINAL ANALYSIS
======================================================================

1. Emotion Classification
The best emotion classification model was DistilBERT with Macro-F1 0.8862 and accuracy 0.9200.
DistilBERT improved Macro-F1 over Linear SVM by +0.0273.
However, Linear SVM required far less training compute, while DistilBERT provided stronger contextual representations and better minority-class recall.

2. Static Embeddings
Average GloVe embeddings with Logistic Regression achieved Macro-F1 0.3684. This was weaker than TF-IDF models because averaging static word vectors loses word order and contextual information.

3. N-gram Language Modelling
Backoff perplexity improved from unigram 548.5972 to bigram 216.9287 and trigram 196.7952. The trigram backoff model provided the lowest perplexity.

4. Sequence Labelling
The HMM + Viterbi POS tagger achieved accuracy 0.8590.
The CRF achieved strict entity-level F1 0.7871 for NER.

5. Topic Modelling
BERTopic obtained the highest coherence score (0.1502). BERTopic also produced more human-interpretable semantic clusters than LSA or LDA.

6. Retrieval-Augmented Generation
The in-domain query was successfully answered using hybrid sparse and dense retrieval.
For the out-of-knowledge query, retrieval confidence was low, so generation was blocked and the system returned 'I do not have enough information.' This reduced the risk of hallucination.

7. Accuracy vs Compute vs Interpretability
Naive Bayes was simple and highly interpretable but weaker on minority classes.
Logistic Regression and Linear SVM provided strong performance with low computational cost and were much faster than Transformer training.
DistilBERT achieved the best overall classification performance but required substantially more compute and training time.
Therefore, Linear SVM offers an excellent accuracy-efficiency trade-off, while DistilBERT is preferred when maximum classification performance and contextual understanding are more important.
